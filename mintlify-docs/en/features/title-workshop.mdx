The Title Workshop is used to generate, compare, and adjust book titles or chapter titles. It is not just about picking a nice-sounding name: it helps you judge whether a title accurately conveys the work's selling points, the protagonist's situation, reader expectations, and reasons to click.

## Generate Book Title Candidates

Open the **Title Workshop** (标题工坊) in the left-side "Creation" group. After entering or selecting the current work direction, the system will generate a batch of title candidates. Before generating, it is best to prepare the following information so the candidates are more likely to fit:

- Genre and target readers
- Protagonist identity or core situation
- Core selling points and emotional promise
- The story's main conflict or payoff
- Title styles you do not want, for example too literary, too abstract, or too much like old web-novel formulas

<Info>
  The more specific the information, the stronger the genre feel and selling-point feel of the title candidates. When only a vague direction is given, titles tend to fall into generic formulas.
</Info>

Generation results usually cover multiple naming directions, for example from protagonist identity, core conflict, golden finger, emotional promise, contrast setting, or world rules, so candidates differ from one another.

## Compare and Filter

After receiving candidates, first exclude titles that do not fit the work, then compare the remaining options across four items:

| Dimension | What a good title shows |
| --- | --- |
| Genre feel | At a glance you can judge the story type, for example fantasy, urban, suspense |
| Selling-point feel | Hints at the protagonist's advantage, core conflict, or reader payoff |
| Emotional intensity | The title itself carries anticipation, tension, or curiosity |
| Recognizability | Not easily confused with other works, has a memorable point |

Prioritize the title that best explains the protagonist's situation, core selling point, and genre temperament. If a title sounds nice but does not show what the book is about, it is usually not the optimal choice.

<Tip>
  When multiple titles are good, return to the work direction and confirm: which title best represents the core experience you intend to deliver to readers.
</Tip>

## Targeted Fine-Tuning

On candidates close to the target, you can continue targeted adjustments. When fine-tuning, you can explicitly tell the system:

- Whether you want stronger or weaker emotional impact
- Whether you want a more direct or more suspenseful expression
- Whether you want to highlight protagonist identity or highlight world rules
- Which already-appearing formulas you want to avoid

<Warning>
  Titles need to attract readers, but they must also match the work's ability to deliver. Excessive exaggeration creates wrong reader expectations, making subsequent chapters harder to sustain.
</Warning>

If a batch of titles is too similar in formula and repeats information, switch to a different naming direction to regenerate, rather than repeatedly fine-tuning in the same direction.

## Chapter Title Optimization

The Title Workshop also supports adjusting chapter titles. The most important function of a chapter title is to serve reading rhythm: it can hint at this chapter's conflict, twist, payoff, or suspense, and does not need to chase a strong clickbait effect every chapter.

Before optimizing chapter titles, confirm three pieces of information for this chapter:

1. **Chapter task**: What the protagonist aims to achieve in this chapter
2. **Core conflict**: The main opposition or pressure in this chapter
3. **Ending hook**: The reason left at the chapter end for readers to continue to the next chapter

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm this chapter's task and conflict">
    Locate this chapter's core action in the chapter plan or breakdown results.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate title candidates">
    Input the task, conflict, and hook, and let the system generate titles around reading rhythm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the title that best prompts read-on motivation">
    Prioritize titles that let readers quickly judge this chapter's highlight while retaining a little suspense.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Position in the Creation Pipeline

Titles affect the user's first impression of a work and also affect how the system understands the book's main direction. A clear title helps subsequent synopsis, project settings, cover prompts, and chapter planning stay aligned on the same selling point.

The Title Workshop is commonly used at three positions:

- **Before starting a book**: Finding the book title direction
- **After Auto Director**: Supplementing the title around the selected direction
- **After chapter breakdown**: Polishing chapter titles

After selecting the book title, return to novel creation or project settings to use it. Chapter titles are replaced directly in chapter editing.
